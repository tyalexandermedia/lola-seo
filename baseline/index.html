Free mobility baseline

Three checks for your ankles, hips and knees. It takes about five minutes at home. Write your numbers down, then retest every few weeks to see what's changing.

This isn't a medical assessment. Stop if anything hurts, and talk to a doctor or physical therapist about pain.

The three checks.

Do them in this order, both sides. Go slowly. You're looking for an honest starting point.

  1. Ankles: knee-to-wall

    Demo video coming soon
    What you need
    A wall, bare feet, and a tape measure or ruler.
    How to do it
    1. Face the wall with one foot pointing straight at it, big toe a few centimeters back.
    2. Keep your heel down and drive that knee straight forward over your toes until it touches the wall.
    3. If it touches with the heel down, slide the foot back a little and go again.
    4. Find the farthest spot where the knee still touches and the heel stays down.
    5. Measure from your big toe to the wall. Repeat on the other side.
    What to record
    The distance for each side, in centimeters or inches.
    What to notice
    A big difference between left and right, the heel lifting, or the knee caving inward instead of tracking over the toes.

  2. Hips: seated rotation

    Demo video coming soon
    What you need
    A chair or box high enough that your knees sit at about 90 degrees.
    How to do it
    1. Sit tall near the front edge with your thighs parallel and your feet off the floor or light on it.
    2. Keep the thigh still and swing the shin out to the side. That is internal rotation.
    3. Come back to the middle, then swing the shin in across your body. That is external rotation.
    4. Stop each one when the thigh lifts or your pelvis shifts. Only count what the hip does on its own.
    5. Repeat on the other side.
    What to record
    For each direction on each side, pick one: limited (barely moves, or you have to lean to get there), okay (moves, but less than you would like or less than the other side), or free (moves easily and nothing else shifts).
    What to notice
    Differences from side to side, and any pinching at the front of the hip. Stay out of anything that pinches.

  3. Knees: controlled step-down

    Demo video coming soon
    What you need
    A low step, about 15 to 20 cm (6 to 8 inches). Stand near a wall or rail in case you need a hand for balance.
    How to do it
    1. Stand on the step on one leg with the other foot hanging off the edge.
    2. Bend the standing knee and lower the other heel slowly, taking about three seconds, until it taps the floor.
    3. Tap lightly without putting weight on it, then stand back up.
    4. Do 5 reps, then switch legs.
    What to record
    For each leg, pick one: steady, some wobble, or knee caves in or hips drop.
    What to notice
    Whether the standing knee stays over the foot and the hips stay level. Filming from the front makes this much easier to see.
